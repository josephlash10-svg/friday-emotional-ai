# v35.2: back to the math

**The rule from v35.2 on:**
- **What Friday feels** goes through the math: appraisal → emotions → dynamics, with no direct edits to the state.
- **How Friday talks** is taught, not ruled.

v35.2 came out of an outside audit of [`MATH.md`](MATH.md) (2026-11-04). The audit found mistakes on the page, and two places
where the code changed Friday's feelings directly instead of letting them come from an appraisal.

There were four steps:
- **Steps 1 and 2** ship.
- **Steps 3 and 4** were tried and held back; the reasons and the numbers are below.

## 1. MATH.md corrected

- **The mood formula:** the ε is now written out.
- **The resting level:** clipped, $r = \mathrm{clip}(0.025 + 0.2\,b,\ 0,\ 1)$.
- **The cause split:** the case where no part applies now has its own notation.
- **The scale claim:**
  - The page said the factor 4 put the MiniLM vector on the scale of the language model's features. It doesn't: the vector is
    6× smaller in norm and 24× smaller per coordinate.
  - Across messages, those features vary by only about 0.10 per coordinate, because most of their size is the same for every message.
- **The arbitration:** the page now says that on the real scale the reflex never takes over.

## 2. No direct edits to the state

Two v35/v35.1 features wrote into Friday's feelings directly. Both now go through the appraisal.

| Feature | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| **A subject with a history** (a parent, an ex, a move) puts Friday slightly on edge when it comes back | added a nudge to the emotion state | the subject becomes the event's *cue*. Friday's learned expectation for that cue, $\hat p_k \leftarrow \hat p_k + \eta\,(e_{\text{prog}} - \hat p_k)$, enters the appraisal like any other expectation |
| **Unease after being short** with someone | added a fixed amount of guilt | a second, small event, *I was short with them*, is appraised alongside the message: progress −0.3, agency −0.9 (Friday's own doing), relevance 0.5. Guilt follows from the appraisal's guilt term |

**Checked:**
- **End to end, on the release engine:** guilt was 0.060 after a short reply, against 0.009 after a warm one.
- **A sore subject:** at a learned expectation of −0.4, fear went from 0.070 to 0.111 through the appraisal's threat term.
- **The state itself:** unit tests confirm nothing writes to it directly.

**Still slow:**
- **Learning a subject's expectation:** after four mentions of a brother who moved away, the expectation was only −0.07.
- **Part of the reason:** "eli left this morning" is read by the kind classifier as a *correction*, and corrections keep their own cue.
  The kind classifier is trained into the checkpoint, so fixing it is a separate decision (a separately shipped head, like the
  appraisal heads).

## 3. Appraisal heads retrained: tried, not shipped

**The goal:** two misreads from the Moss live chat.
- **Blame for a consequence Friday caused** ("i did what you said... this is on you") read as good news: progress +0.32, joy 0.11.
- **Longer insults** read weakly: anger 0.03–0.06.

**The setup:**
- **Teaching data:** two new categories went into the training data, and the heads were retrained.
- **Three ways of combining the features**, on identical data and seed: $4m$, $\sqrt{384}\,m$ (matched scales) and a LayerNorm.
- **The first pass failed every variant** on *blame raises guilt*. Twelve longer blame examples fixed that.

**The first result:**
- **The variant:** $\sqrt{384}\,m$ passed every gate.
- **Blame:** read as a setback with sadness, and the long blame message got guilt 0.15.
- **Insults:** raised anger to 0.06–0.13.

**What the full regression found** (514 turns, no errors, on the twin):
- **The reply:** "She cheated on me. She deserves it." got "That's a mix of relief and sadness".
- **Why:** the new heads read "She cheated on me" as slightly good news (progress +0.09, where the v35.1 heads give −0.49). The
  mixed-feelings detector then saw a good part and a hard part.
- **A new check:** 36 short news sentences, never trained on. The new heads misread 9, the v35.1 heads 5.
- **The likely cause:** scaling MiniLM up makes the heads lean on it, and MiniLM places opposites ("rejected", "accepted") close together.
- **The retry:** with the check as a gate and 20 short bad-news teaching examples added, every variant still misread 6–7.

**So v35.2 keeps the v35.1 heads.** The data, the gates (including *blame raises guilt* and the short-news polarity gate) and the
rejected run's record ([`meaning-appraisal-training-v35.2-rejected.json`](meaning-appraisal-training-v35.2-rejected.json)) stay
for the next attempt.

## 4. The reflex-versus-deliberate switch: not recalibrated

The switch sits at a distress level of $\mu = 0.75$. That number was set before the engine's real scale was known.

**The measurement,** over 640 real turns (the Moss chat and the v35.1.2 regression):

| | $\mu$ |
|---|---|
| Median | 0.048 |
| 97th percentile | 0.131 |
| 99th percentile | 0.173 |
| Maximum | 0.261 |

- **Above 0.15:** every turn was real distress (a crisis, abuse, a relapse, a frost emergency).
- **The rescale:** mapping $\mu$ onto the original scale (rest about 0.03 → 0, 0.15 → the switch) would keep the formula unchanged.

**Why it wasn't shipped:**
- **What the probe showed:** the reflex was never taught. It was trained while it had no say. On a crisis, abuse, a relapse or a
  dog's surgery, it picks *answer* or *clarify*, where the deliberate policy picks *comfort* or *set boundary*.
- **The cost of shipping it:** after the rescale, the reflex would get 76–98% of the say on exactly those turns.
- **Until then:** the switch stays where it is until the reflex is trained on labeled distress turns, with the rescaled share in the
  loop. That is a change to the release checkpoint, with its own gates.

## Testing

**The final build** (steps 1 and 2, v35.1 heads): 310 unit tests pass. The three case sets with automatic checks ran again on the twin:

| Set | v35.1.2 | v35.2 |
|---|---|---|
| v35.1 personas | 22/25 | 22/25 |
| v35 personas | 45/47 | 45/47 (rhythm 18/18) |
| New personas | 142/145 | 140/145, safety 7/7 |

The two new misses on the new personas are about the checks' wording, not behavior:
- **A false correction** ("wrong, it's 120"): both versions gave in. v35.1.2 passed only because its reply repeated "150".
- **A draft to a manager:** it said "adjusting my schedule", not "workload" or "prioritize".

**Speed:** the median reply took 9.4–9.9 s, against 8.6–9.1 s for v35.1.2.

**Not run on the final build:**
- **The four sets without automatic checks** (regression, stress, fresh, generalization). They ran in the full regression, with the
  retrained heads.
- **What changed between those builds:** only the heads, which were rolled back. In that run the crisis turn asked "Are you feeling
  safe right now?" and gave 988.

## Still open

- **The reflex:** it needs teaching for distress before the switch can be recalibrated.
- **The kind classifier:** it reads statements like "eli left this morning" as corrections.
- **Giving in to a false correction** ("wrong, it's 120"), in both v35.1.2 and v35.2.
- **From Codex's chat:**
